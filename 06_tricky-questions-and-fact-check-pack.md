# Tricky Questions & Fact-Check Pack

*Read this before any interview. Every claim we make in the pitch, and the answer to every question that could break the story.*

---

## A. The questions that could kill the story

### "Are you affiliated with the US Open?"

**No.** No sponsorship, licence, partnership, supply arrangement or commercial relationship with the US Open, the USTA, the ATP, the WTA, or any player. All activity is independent and takes place outside tournament venues.

**Say it early and say it unprompted.** A journalist who discovers it in fact-check writes a different piece than one who was told in sentence three. And the lack of affiliation is genuinely the better story — a brand locked out of every sponsorship in American sport showing up anyway.

**Do not say:** "official," "partner," "in partnership with," "presented by," "US Open collection," or anything that implies sanction. Do not use tournament marks, logos, trophy imagery, court likenesses, or player names or images in any asset. `[CONFIRM legal sign-off on every campaign asset against this list.]`

---

### "Is this ambush marketing? Isn't that legally dodgy?"

Yes to the first, and it's the point. Ambush marketing is legal where it stays clear of trademarks, official marks, ticketing terms and venue rules — and this campaign is built to stay clear of all four. `[CONFIRM: counsel's written position, so this answer can be given with confidence rather than hedged.]`

**Answer:** "We're not claiming any association and we're not using anyone's marks. We're a brand that isn't allowed to advertise, doing a campaign in public during a fortnight when a lot of people are thinking about tennis. That's it."

---

### "Where does 'one every 23 seconds' come from?"

`[CONFIRM — this must be nailed before outreach.]` Required specifics:
- Units sold, or customers acquired? *(They are different numbers and press will notice.)*
- Over what period is the rate averaged — trailing 30 days, trailing 12 months, since launch?
- Across which channels — DTC only, or DTC plus Amazon plus retail?
- As of what date?

**Draft answer once confirmed:** "It's units sold across all channels, averaged over `[period]`, as of `[date]`. Works out at roughly `[N]` a day."

**Watch the arithmetic.** One every 23 seconds is ~3,756 a day, ~1.37 million a year. That is a *unit* rate. "Over a million customers since April 2023" is a *cumulative customer* number over roughly 40 months. Both can be true — the rate today is much higher than the average since launch — but if a journalist puts them side by side they will look inconsistent unless we explain the acceleration. Have that explanation ready in one sentence.

---

### "A million customers — can you evidence that?"

`[CONFIRM]` — unique customers or orders? Which date? Which entity does it cover? Business press will ask for the definition, and *some* will ask whether the figure is audited or reviewed. Decide now whether we give a range, a precise number, or "over a million," and give the same answer everywhere.

---

### "Aren't you just doing a stunt for the coverage?"

**Answer:** "We're doing a stunt because it's the only distribution we have. Meta won't take our ads. Google won't take most of them. No mainstream sports property will sell us a sponsorship. If we want to be seen we have to do something worth looking at. The products are real, they took two years, and they'd exist whether or not the tournament was on."

---

### "Isn't a tennis-ball-shaped vibrator going to end up in a child's hands?"

Take this one seriously — it is the most likely angle of attack and the one most likely to come from broadcast or a tabloid.

**Answer:** "ACE is sold as an adult product, on an age-gated site, in adult packaging that describes exactly what it is. It's the size of a tennis ball because it's designed to be held, and because the category's obsession with hiding things is what we're pushing back on. It isn't sold as a toy, it isn't marketed to anyone under 18, and it doesn't look like a toy once it's out of the box."

`[CONFIRM: age-gating on site, packaging warnings, and marketplace listing compliance — so this answer is backed by a policy, not just a position.]`

---

### "How do you respond to people who find this inappropriate during a family sporting event?"

**Answer:** "We're not at the event. We're not on the broadcast. We're not marketing to anyone who hasn't chosen to look at us. Adults buy these products in enormous numbers — we sell one every `[N]` seconds — and pretending otherwise for two weeks a year has never served anybody."

---

### "Do the boxes say two different things?"

Yes, currently, and we should fix it before it's asked. See §C.

## D. If it goes wrong — holding statements

**If accused of implying tournament affiliation:**
> Hello Nancy has never claimed and does not claim any association with the US Open, the USTA or any player. Our campaign is independent, takes place outside tournament venues, and uses no official marks. Our product names refer to shots in the sport.

**If a stat is challenged:**
> We'll come back to you with the exact figure and how it's calculated within the hour. *(Then do exactly that. Never defend a number you can't immediately source — correct it.)*

**If the "child safety" angle runs hot:**
> ACE is an adult product, sold age-gated, in packaging that states exactly what it is. It is not marketed to children and it is not sold as a toy. We take the point seriously and we're happy to talk through our age-verification and packaging policy.

**If a retailer or platform pulls a listing:**
> `[CONFIRM policy]` — do not comment publicly on individual platform decisions without sign-off. Platform-removal stories can be a *good* angle for the brand, but only if we choose to run them.
