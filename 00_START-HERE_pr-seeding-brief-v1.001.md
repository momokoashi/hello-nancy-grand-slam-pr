## 1. The one-paragraph version

Hello Nancy makes sexual wellness products that look like fruit. A lemon. A raspberry. An avocado. Nothing you'd hide in a drawer. In three years the brand has gone from zero to over a million customers and now sells a product roughly **every 23 seconds**. This month it launches its first two products that aren't food: **ACE**, an air-suction massager shaped like a tennis ball, and **SMASH**, a wand vibrator built around the most powerful shot in the sport. And then — without a sponsorship, a licence, or an invitation — it is going to run the entire US Open.

---

## 2. Why press should care (in the order they'll care)

**Because a sexual wellness brand is ambushing the biggest tournament in America.** Not with a booth. Not with a licensing deal. With two products, a fortnight of street-level activity in New York, and a name on the box that reads *GAME. SET. FINISH.* This is the story most editors will want first, and it is not really a product story — it's a marketing story about a category that isn't allowed to advertise doing an end-run around a sport that would never sell it a sponsorship.

**Because the design thesis is genuinely different.** Every other brand in this category solves for discretion: matte black, hidden, "looks like a lipstick." Hello Nancy solves for the opposite. Its products are designed to be *left out*. A lemon on the nightstand. A tennis ball on the shelf. That is a real design position, it is three years old and consistent, and it is why the brand grew the way it did.

**Because the growth number is a business story on its own.** Zero to a million-plus customers since April 2023, in a category with almost no paid-media access — no Meta, no Google, restricted TikTok, no TV. That growth had to come from product, packaging and word of mouth. Business and retail press can write that story without ever mentioning what the products do.

**Because tennis is a fashion and culture moment right now,** and this is the only entry into it from a category nobody expected. Tenniscore has been running for two years across fashion, film and beauty. This is the first time it has produced something for the nightstand.

**Because there's a line in the sport that writes itself.** Tennis is the only game in the world where a score of zero is called *love*. Hello Nancy's entire campaign is built on correcting that.

## 3. The line we want quoted

> **In tennis, love means zero. We think that's the problem.**

Supporting lines, all quotable, all clean enough for broadcast:

- **"Game. Set. Finish."** — on the ACE box, the campaign's headline.
- **"The most powerful shot in tennis. Now in your nightstand."** — on the SMASH box.
- **"The score only moves in one direction. Yours."** — ACE copy.
- **"Nothing you'd hide in a drawer."** — the brand thesis in five words.
- **"We sell one every 23 seconds. Nobody has ever seen an ad for us."** — the growth line.

Use these consistently. One repeated line across forty placements builds a brand; forty clever lines build nothing.

## 4. The two products, in press language

### ACE — Air Suction Massager
A tennis ball, in the hand, that produces gentle waves of air rather than contact. Twelve intensities. Whisper quiet, fully waterproof, USB-C, body-safe silicone. Positioned as **beginner-friendly**: the entry point, the one you buy first, the one you can leave on a shelf and nobody asks.
**Colourways: tennis-ball green and pink.**
Box copy: *Gentle air. Nothing else.*

### SMASH — Wand Vibrator
A wand with a deep, rumbly motor and a flexible head that keeps the power exactly where you put it. Eight modes across eight intensities, whisper quiet, fully waterproof, USB-C, body-safe silicone. Positioned as **the powerful one**: for people who already know how much they can handle.
**Colourway: tennis-ball green only.**
Box copy: *The most powerful shot in tennis. Now in your nightstand.*

**The pair works as a story:** ACE is the serve you can't touch, SMASH is the shot you can't return. Beginner and expert. Air and power. One product for the person entering the category and one for the person who's been in it for a decade. Most brands launch one thing; launching the pair lets an editor write a comparison, which is the format that actually converts in service journalism and gift guides.

## 7. What we're offering press — the seeding structure

Three tiers. Full kit spec, pack-out and shipping cut-offs in `07_seeding-tiers-and-kit-spec.md`.

**Tier 1 — Exclusive (2–3 outlets max).**
First look under embargo, founder interview, both products in both colourways, the campaign's full asset set including anything unreleased, and the option to attend the Cancun residency. In exchange: publish on the embargo lift, and cover the campaign, not just the product.

**Tier 2 — Priority (25–40).**
Both products, full press kit, quotes on request, 48h response guarantee, gift-guide-ready imagery and copy blocks. This is the tier that produces most of the coverage.

**Tier 3 — Broad (100+ / creator wave).**
ACE only, in the colourway that suits the recipient, with the one-page fact sheet and a link. No ask attached. In this category an unrequested "post this" is the fastest way to get nothing.

**The rule for all three: no posting quotas, no approval process.** Category coverage that reads as bought reads as bought. The kit is good enough to earn the post.

## 8. What makes the kit worth opening

The unboxing is the campaign for a lot of recipients, so build it like an object, not a mailer:

- A tennis-ball tube — the round three-ball can, but it opens to ACE.
- Both products in the tennis-ball green, SMASH's flexible head visible.
- A scorecard insert, hand-numbered, with the *love means zero* line on it.
- A single printed card with the specs and the URL. Nothing else to read.
- Personalisation on the outer, not inside — that's what gets photographed at the door.

## 9. Guardrails — how we don't lose the story

**Never lead with the product's function.** Lead with the ambush, the design thesis, or the growth number. Every editor in this category has been pitched a vibrator this week; none of them has been pitched a brand doing the US Open without permission.

**Never claim a tournament affiliation.** Hello Nancy is not a sponsor, partner, licensee or supplier to the US Open, the USTA, or any player. Every piece of outreach must be unambiguous about this, and every asset must avoid marks, logos, and official language. The campaign is *about* tennis; it is not *of* the tournament. This is the single biggest legal and reputational exposure in the campaign — see `06_tricky-questions-and-fact-check-pack.md`.

**Never send AI-generated imagery as product photography.** The `99_concept-renders_DO-NOT-PUBLISH` folder is mood and internal reference only. Press-ready assets are listed in `09_asset-manifest-and-shot-list.md`; anything not on that list does not leave the building.

**Never use a statistic we can't source.** The fact-check pack has the sourced numbers. If a stat isn't in it, it isn't ours to use.

**Don't oversell "one every 23 seconds" without the definition.** It means units sold, not customers, and press will ask. Have the definition ready in the same sentence — see §10 and `06`.

**Keep the tone of the box.** The packaging is funny, plain-spoken, and never coy. Press materials that go clinical or go smutty both break the brand. The register is: confident, warm, slightly amused, and never apologising for the category.
