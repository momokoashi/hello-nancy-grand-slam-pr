# Pitch Emails & Subject Lines — ACE & SMASH

*Copy-paste ready. Fill the `[CONFIRM]` fields, personalise the first sentence, send. Never send more than one angle per journalist.*

---

## Rules before you send anything

1. **Personalise the first sentence or don't send it.** Reference something they actually wrote. Everything after sentence one can be templated; sentence one cannot.
2. **The subject line is the pitch.** If it doesn't work as a headline, the pitch doesn't work.
3. **Never attach files.** One link to the press kit. Attachments get filtered and nobody opens a 40 MB email.
4. **Never lead with the product.** Lead with the ambush, the design thesis, or the growth number.
5. **One follow-up, five working days later, on a different angle.** Then stop. A third email costs you the relationship for the next launch too.
6. **Say the affiliation line early.** "We're not a sponsor — that's the point" is the hook, and it also pre-empts the question that would otherwise kill the story in fact-check.

Its products are shaped like fruit, which is a design decision, not a gimmick: every other brand in the category solves for hiding, and this one solves for leaving things out.

## Pitch 4 — The Fruit Bowl Thesis
*For: Dezeen, Wallpaper, Fast Company Design, It's Nice That, Cool Hunting, Design Milk, Creative Review*

> **Subject:** Everyone else in this category designs for hiding. We design for leaving out.
>
> Hi `[NAME]`,
>
> `[PERSONALISED FIRST LINE]`
>
> There's a design orthodoxy in sexual wellness that nobody has ever really challenged: the product should be discreet. Matte black, neutral, "looks like a lipstick." Every brand in the category is solving the same brief — how do we make this easy to hide?
>
> Hello Nancy inverted the brief. Its products are a lemon, a raspberry, an avocado — objects designed to sit on a nightstand in plain sight and read as decor. The premise is that the shame is in the hiding, and if the object is charming enough there's nothing to hide.
>
> This month it applies the thesis to sport. **ACE** is an air-suction massager built to a real tennis ball's dimensions — 65 mm, 110 g, felt-green — so that on a shelf it simply *is* a tennis ball. **SMASH** is a wand with a head sleeve carrying the same sun-texture as new felt.
>
> The packaging is worth a look on its own: because the brand can't buy advertising, the box carries the campaign. `[CONFIRM: link to packaging assets]`
>
> Happy to send the artwork, the product, and the designer on the record about designing for visibility rather than concealment.
>
> `[SIGN-OFF]`
