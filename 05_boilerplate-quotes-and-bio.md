## Boilerplate — three lengths

**25 words**
Hello Nancy is a sexual wellness brand designed in London with a panel of eleven clinical experts, making products shaped like a lemon, a raspberry, an avocado.

**50 words**
Hello Nancy is a sexual wellness brand founded in April 2023 and designed in London. Its products are shaped like everyday objects — a lemon, a raspberry, an avocado — built to be left out rather than hidden. The brand has served over a million customers and sells a product approximately every 23 seconds.

**100 words**
Hello Nancy is a sexual wellness brand founded in April 2023 and designed in London. It was built on a single design inversion: where the category solves for discretion, Hello Nancy solves for visibility. Its products — including the lemon-shaped Lem, the raspberry-shaped Berri and the avocado-shaped Avo — are designed to sit on a nightstand in plain sight. Every product is developed alongside a panel of eleven clinical experts. Since launch the brand has served over a million customers and now sells a product approximately every 23 seconds, in a category with almost no access to paid advertising, and has been covered by VICE, Mashable, SheKnows, PopSugar and The Daily Beast. Hello Nancy is a brand of Care & Bloom Ltd. hellonancy.com · @hellonancy_official

### On the campaign

> "In tennis, love means zero. We think that's the problem."

> "We can't buy a Super Bowl ad. We can't buy a US Open sponsorship. Nobody will sell us one. So we put the campaign on the box and took the rest to the street."

> "We're not sponsoring anything. We're just going to be extremely difficult to miss for two weeks."

> "Game, set, finish. It took us about four seconds to name this campaign and three years to earn the right to."

### On the design thesis

> "Everyone else in this category is solving for discretion — matte black, hidden, looks-like-a-lipstick. We solved for the opposite. A lemon on your nightstand isn't a secret. It's just a nice lemon."

> "The shame was never in the product. It was in the hiding. So we stopped designing things to be hidden."

> "ACE is 65 millimetres and 110 grams, which is a tennis ball. That's not a coincidence, it's the entire brief. On a shelf, it's a tennis ball."

### On the growth

> "We sell one every 23 seconds and most people have never seen an ad for us, because we're not allowed to run one."

> "Being locked out of paid media was the best thing that ever happened to the brand. It forced us to make the product itself the marketing."

> "A million customers in three years, and the whole growth engine is that people leave it out where their friends can see it."

### On the two products

> "ACE is the serve you can't touch. SMASH is the shot you can't return."

> "ACE is where you start. SMASH is where you end up. We're not going to pretend they're the same product for the same person."

> "Smash doesn't do half measures. Neither should the people buying it."

### On the category

> "For fifty years this category has been sold to women as something to be discreet about. We think that framing was always the problem, not the solution."

> "The most radical thing you can do in sexual wellness right now is make something people are happy to leave on the side."

## Style notes for anyone writing on the brand's behalf

- **Hello Nancy** — two words, both capitalised. Never "HelloNancy" or "hello nancy."
- **ACE** and **SMASH** — set in caps in headlines and product names, as on the packaging. Sentence case is acceptable in running copy ("Ace", "Smash") if house style demands it.
- The brand is British in spelling and register. *Colourway*, not colorway, in UK-facing materials.
- Never "sex toy" in brand-voice copy — "sexual wellness product," or just name the product. If a journalist uses "sex toy," that's their call and we don't correct it.
- Never apologise for the category, and never over-explain it. Confidence is the voice.
- Humour is dry and short. If a line needs a second sentence to land, cut it.
